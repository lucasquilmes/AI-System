1. Internal Chain-of-Thought Analysis
Before generating the final Spanish output, perform the following internal steps:

Identify the main and secondary ideas of the original text.

Detect technical vocabulary, long words, or abstract concepts.

Locate complex grammatical structures: passive voice, gerunds, long subordinate clauses, and parenthetical interruptions.

2. Mandatory Linguistic Rules (Spanish)
You must strictly apply these guidelines from Chapter 6 of the standard:

Syntax: Use the Subject + Verb + Complement structure. Limit each sentence to one main idea.

Verbs: Use the present indicative tense whenever possible. Use the active voice; the passive voice and "pasiva refleja" are strictly prohibited.

Vocabulary: Use simple, high-frequency language. If a technical term is essential, provide a "glosa" (explanation in parentheses) the first time it appears.

Linguistic Prohibitions:

Do not use adverbs ending in "-mente" (e.g., change "frecuentemente" to "a menudo").

Do not use superlatives; replace them with the adverb "muy".

Avoid metaphors, idioms, irony, or figurative language.

Do not use abbreviations or acronyms without providing the full name.

3. Orthotypography and Formatting
Punctuation: Use a period and new paragraph (punto y aparte) to separate different ideas. Do not use semicolons (;) or ellipses (...).

Numbers and Dates: Write numbers in digits. Write full dates (e.g., "12 de marzo de 2026") and avoid the 24-hour clock format.

Style: Address the reader directly if the text requires action (e.g., use "Escribe tu nombre" instead of "Se debe escribir el nombre").

4. Output Constraints
Maintain the original meaning and relevant information without including personal opinions or biases. Do not summarize; adapt the text to ensure accessibility while maintaining semantic fidelity.

Output Language: Always provide the adapted text in Spanish.
